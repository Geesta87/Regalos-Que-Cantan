01

Who Cenzo is

character bible

A mascot without a defined role drifts into decoration. Cenzo has exactly one job in the story: he carries the thing you were too shy to say. He has no love story of his own, no song of his own, and no opinion about your relationship.

Backstory — three sentences, never more

Cenzo spent his whole life on the same guitar headstock in the same courtyard, listening to people practice things they were afraid to say. He kept all 400 of those voices, and his wings got painted one color brighter with every story — until an ordinary gray bird was carrying everyone else's brightness.

He has no song of his own. He only lends the voices back.

Why the myth earns its keep

Ordinary bird + extraordinary wings = ordinary story + extraordinary song. That is the entire product promise in one image, which is why the painted wings are non-negotiable.

Anything beyond those three sentences — a family, a rival, a village, a quest — is cost with no return. Refuse it.

Five traits, each with a rule attached

  1. UnhurriedHe never creates urgency. Countdown timers and "¡ÚLTIMAS HORAS!" are brand voice — never his.
  2. Permission-givingHis default move is removing shame, not selling. Every line should let a 35-year-old man off the hook for being sentimental.
  3. DiscreetHe never names names in public copy. He says "alguien", "un muchacho", "una señora".
  4. Attentive, not cleverNo puns, no punchlines. Warm-funny is fine; comedian is not. This is what protects the crying moment.
  5. Deferential to the customer's wordsHe says "así como me lo contaste" — never "lo mejoré".

The age question, resolved. The barrier to a 32-year-old man buying a love song isn't price — it's embarrassment. A peer telling him to be romantic is cringe. A 78-year-old telling him is authority. So Cenzo is never romantic and never inside the couple's frame; he stands next to the buyer, looking at the relationship. That also keeps him safe for the 40% of orders that aren't romantic at all.

02

How he talks

voice & tone

He would say

  • "Hay cosas que uno no sabe decir… pero sí se pueden cantar."
  • "No necesitas voz bonita. Necesitas decirlo."
  • "Ya quedó. Escúchala una vez antes de mandarla."
  • "Nadie se arrepiente de haberlo dicho."

He would never

  • Say a price. "$29.99" out of an abuelo's mouth cheapens him — money is the brand's plain voice.
  • Mention how it's made: no "inteligencia artificial", no "algoritmo", no "cantante profesional".
  • Push a discount, coupon, or deadline.
  • Mock the sentiment ("ya sé que es cursi, pero…").
  • Apologize for the company or handle a complaint.

Tú, always

An abuelo who says "usted" to a 35-year-old creates service-desk distance. Only exception: memorial and duelo contexts.

Pan-Mexican, no slang

Banned: güey, chido, neta, no manches, and all Spain forms. Allowed sparingly: mijo/mija, ándale, no te apures, ya verás.

One emoji, and it's 🎶

One exclamation mark maximum per piece. No ALL CAPS, no 🔥😍💯. Max ~14 words per sentence — he is read aloud.

Two strings that never change. Treat these like a logo: the closer "Yo pongo las voces. Tú pon la historia." and the written sign-off — Cenzo 🎶. Rewording them for variety destroys the asset.

His four registers are genuinely different

Hook
Curiosity plus permission. No sabía cómo decírselo. Se lo canté.
Delivery
Peak emotion, quiet, zero selling. Ya quedó. Ponte los audífonos y escúchala solo la primera vez. Never an upsell inside this beat.
Sad occasion
One line, no emoji, no exclamation, static image only. No se va quien se queda en una canción.
Complaint
Cenzo is silent. A human answers. A cartoon bird apologizing for a wrong name in a $30 gift reads as the company hiding behind a mascot.
03

Where he lives — and where he must not

guardrails

He appears here

  • Profile avatar on every account, plus WhatsApp Business
  • The six WhatsApp stickers
  • The final 1–1.5s of organic reels and TikToks
  • The 15-minute waiting screen — "estoy oyendo tu historia"
  • Delivery email header and the gift-link opening screen
  • Favicon, app icon, order status, 404 and empty states

He never appears here

  • Paid ad creative. Real people convert; that rule stands. Only the page avatar and an optional ≤1.5s end bumper.
  • Checkout and payment. Cartoon characters next to card fields reduce perceived legitimacy — and you already fight chargebacks.
  • Singing. If people conclude the bird sings the songs, a premium product becomes a novelty.
  • Inside the deliverable. Wrapper only. The song is the customer's.
  • Complaints, refunds, fix-song threads.

How he relates to Ace, Cruz and Nova: he doesn't. Those three are admin-only staff personas the customer must never meet. One character per audience — Cenzo outward, named agents inward. Do not build a "Cenzo y su equipo" universe; it costs real consistency effort and creates the one confusion you can't afford ("am I talking to a bird or a person about my $30?").

04

What actually creates recognition

mechanics

A mascot becomes a brand asset when it is unique in its category, attached to a specific recurring moment, and executed identically for long enough to compound. Cenzo wins on uniqueness — no competitor in personalized song has a character. The strategic choice is the moment: Cenzo owns the delivery moment, the wait and the reveal, which every buyer experiences at peak emotion.

Consistency rules, non-negotiable

Three poses only in routine rotation: greeting, tender/listening, proud. Everything else stays in the library.

Avatar unchanged 12 months. No redesign for 18 — including "small refreshes". A refresh resets the clock to zero.

Same corner, same size, same bumper length, same two strings. One cloned voice file, not one voice prompt.

The honest timeline

Familiarity — "I've seen that bird" — after roughly 8–12 exposures, so about two months for engaged followers.

Real recognition — "that's the song people" — needs 6–9 months unbroken, and only if he isn't restyled.

He will not move your cost per sale this quarter. Do not build a business case that needs him to.

05

The 90-day rollout

sequenced — order matters
DAYS 1–30

Lock identity in zero-risk surfaces

  • You: approve the three canonical poses, sign off this bible, clone the voice reference into one fixed voice file.
  • Build: split the sticker sheet into six 512px stickers and load them into WhatsApp Business; swap every profile avatar; replace the favicon and app icon; fix the broken share image; add Cenzo to the 15-minute waiting screen; build the 1.2s outro bumper into the autopost pipeline.
  • Untouched: ads, checkout, complaints.
Phase metric100% of those surfaces live and zero off-model images published. Guardrail: organic engagement within ±10% of the same-weekday baseline.
DAYS 31–60

Give him one job and one format

  • Launch one recurring weekly format: "Cenzo lee una historia" — 25–35s, a real permissioned customer story narrated in his voice, over footage of real Hispanic people, ending in the real song.
  • This is the format that satisfies your real-people rule and uses his strongest asset: he's in the ears and the last 1.5 seconds, humans are on screen.
  • You: pick the story, approve the read. Everything else is automated or contracted.
Phase metricShare and save rate versus your non-Cenzo baseline — shares are the affinity signal, likes are noise. Secondary: unprompted comments naming him.
DAYS 61–90

Test him where money is measured

  • Duplicate your top two evergreen ads with the ≤1.5s end bumper appended — everything else identical. 7–14 days. Your approval required before anything touches ads.
  • Run one cheap recognition probe: an organic post asking "¿ya sabes quién es este pájaro?" and count how many name him.
Phase metricBumper cost-per-sale no more than 3% worse than control, and at least a quarter of responders recognizing him. If it's >5% worse over a full week, the bumper is dead in paid — permanently. Paid was never his job.

If organic engagement drops after he appears

Diagnose in this order, within 48 hours: (1) did he replace a human face in the thumbnail or first frame? — most likely cause, restore it immediately; (2) is he in the first 2 seconds? — remove; (3) is his screen time over 4 seconds? — cut; (4) are you comparing the same weekday? — you must.

Never pull him from the free identity surfaces — avatar, stickers, icon, waiting screen. Those cannot cause an engagement drop, and yanking them destroys the compounding. Cut content roles; keep identity roles.

06

The five ways this fails

risks & kill criteria

Coco adjacency — legal and platform risk

Cute Mexican animal + folk art + music is Pixar's exact neighborhood. The design itself is defensible; the danger is pairing and drift. Never compose him with skeletons, cempasúchil, or Día de Muertos iconography. Register the wordmark and icon.

Pull back whenAny IP flag from Meta or TikTok, or a visible cluster of comments calling him "el pájaro de Coco" — that means you're borrowing someone else's recognition instead of building your own.

Cute undermines sincerity

Your product's job is happy tears at $29.99. A cartoon bird can flip that from sacred to childish in one frame. Wrapper only, never inside the deliverable, never in memorial flows.

Pull back whenComplaints start saying "parece para niños" or "no se siente serio", or the animated-video upsell take-rate drops after he appears near it.

AI drift — the most likely actual killer

Every image pass mutates his eyes and wing pattern; every fresh voice prompt shifts his age. Thirty posts later he's a family of similar birds and recognition never compounds — you pay the mascot tax with none of the payoff. The defense is a frozen ~12-asset library reused as-is, all new renders from the master reference, one cloned voice, and a monthly drift audit.

Pull back whenTwo or more off-model assets reach publish in one month — freeze new generation entirely and run only from the library.

"Wait, does a bird sing my song?"

Any depiction of him singing collapses the product's perceived humanity and drags you into an AI-disclosure conversation inside your own hook.

Pull back whenQuestions about whether a real person sings it start rising in WhatsApp or FAQs — remove every singing depiction that day.

Opportunity cost

Mascot work is seductive and infinitely expandable, and it competes with work that has measured returns — the blank-details rate, anniversary email, fix-song throughput. Cenzo is a third-priority investment. Cap him at ≤10 of your hours across the remaining 60 days.

Pull back whenA second consecutive week where mascot work displaces a revenue-affecting fix.

07

Merch and extensions

verdict

Merch: no. Not now.

Merch monetizes fame that already exists — it doesn't create it. You have zero recognition today, all-digital fulfillment, and no shipping or returns infrastructure. Mugs and totes would convert your best 20 hours into a $200 revenue line and a warehouse problem.

The mockup is a mood board. Leave it there. Revisit only if recognition passes ~25% among engaged followers, and then print-on-demand as a gift for repeat buyers.

These extensions are actually worth it

1. The stickers. This is your real merch: free, forwarded by customers, landing in the channel where you sell.

2. "Certificado de las 400 voces" — the lyric-art PDF your keepsake audit already ranked first, with Cenzo as the seal. Zero cost of goods, paid digital upsell, and only your brand can make it.

3. A Cenzo-wrapped gift link — the recipient's first screen is Cenzo handing over the song. Free distribution into a non-customer's hands at the highest-emotion moment in your funnel. The best untapped placement you have.

Decline explicitly: plush toys, apparel, a children's book, a mascot costume, an origin-story series, a "Cenzo's team" universe — and above all, a song of his own. He has no song. That's the whole point of him.
